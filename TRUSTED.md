## Trusted base (from the design spec)

- rustc, Verus (including its `by (compute)` interpreter), Z3, and vstd's specs of std.
- The OS UDP stack, the clock, and getrandom (used for connection IDs, stateless-reset tokens, and each TLS handshake's `TLS_RAND_LEN` bytes: the ServerHello random, the ECDHE scalar and the PSS salt; also PATH_CHALLENGE data and the spin bit).
- The unverified IO shell and the `main` binary, including loading the certificate chain and parsing the PEM/PKCS#8 P-256 private key (the 4c shell's PEM and PKCS#8 parsing; RSA keys are refused until phase 10a (docs/roadmap.md)).
- **The hand-written Verus formalization of each RFC** (`spec` modules). Proofs show the code matches these specs; they can't show the specs match the RFC text. Keeping the specs small and readable, with each one citing its RFC section, is how the risk stays manageable.
- vstd models a `Vec`'s length, not its capacity, so every proven memory bound (e.g. `quic::reassembly`'s `phys_len() <= 2 * buf_len()` and `RecvStream`'s `buffered_phys()`) bounds lengths only. `Reassembly::pop` releases both allocations on a full drain by construction (fresh `Vec`s, and `split_off` keeps only the tail allocation); the unverified test-only accessors `Reassembly::capacities` and `RecvStream::capacities` (`tests/recv_stream.rs`, `empty_stream_frame_keeps_window`) check it in `tests/reassembly.rs` (`drain_releases_capacity`); likewise `SendStream::on_data_acked` replaces its buffer with a fresh `Vec` once everything written is acknowledged, checked through `SendStream::buf_capacity` in `tests/send_stream.rs` (`ack_releases_capacity`; `ack_copies_linear` uses it to count the bytes its lazy trims copy); and `RecvPns`'s range list holds at most `MAX_ACK_RANGES` + 1 entries at any moment (the lowest is popped in place, never re-allocated), checked through `RecvPns::ranges_capacity` in `tests/ack_track.rs` (`ranges_capacity_bounded`); and `SentPackets::on_ack` and `drain_inflight` leave a fresh `Vec` (the rebuild and the swap), checked through `SentPackets::inflight_capacity` in `tests/sent.rs` (`ack_frees`, `drain_frees`). `SentPackets` itself derives nothing (its ghost `issued` set has no `Debug`). `PeerCids`'s retire lists hold at most `2 * PEER_CID_CAP` entries together (proven in its `wf`), and their capacity is checked through `PeerCids::retire_capacity` in `tests/cid.rs` (`retire_backlog`); its active list holds at most `PEER_CID_CAP` entries (proven), and the Retire Prior To split keeps the tail as a fresh `Vec` (capacity not tested); `PeerCids` derives nothing either (its ghost `received` map and `acked` set have no `Debug`). `CidTable::remove_conn` rebuilds its three lists as fresh `Vec`s (at most `MAX_TABLE` CIDs, `MAX_TOKENS` tokens and `MAX_CONNS` connections, proven in its `wf`), checked through `CidTable::capacities` in `tests/route.rs` (`remove_conn_frees`). `AmpBudget` derives nothing (its ghost trace has no `Debug`), nor do `Path` and `Paths` (they hold one). `Paths` holds at most `MAX_PATHS` paths and each path at most `MAX_RESPONSES` owed responses (proven in its `wf`; the oldest is removed in place), checked through `Paths::capacities` in `tests/path.rs` (`capacity_bounded`). `Connection::discard` gives the discarded space fresh CRYPTO buffers, checked through `Connection::crypto_capacity` (`CryptoStream::capacities` plus the send buffer's `buf_capacity`) in `tests/conn_rx.rs` (`handshake_validates_and_discards` for Initial, `discard_frees_crypto` for Handshake). The out-of-order CRYPTO store (`quic::frags::Frags`, an AVL tree of chunks, `quic::avl`) proves lengths only (bytes held at most 16384 in at most 4096 runs, in `CryptoStream::wf`; each chunk at most `CAP` = 256 bytes and each chunk that starts where another ends at least `K` = 128, in `Frags::wf`). Each chunk is its own `Vec`, requested with `Vec::with_capacity` of its exact length, filled by exactly that many pushes and never grown afterwards, and the lists of chunks `insert` builds on the way (in `gather` and `fill_all`) are requested with room for every entry pushed; each tree node is its own `Box`. That these pushes never reallocate is std's documented guarantee that a push below capacity does not reallocate, trusted: vstd models no capacity, so the proved per-call costs of `Frags::insert` and `take_front` (the `Ghost<nat>` ensures of `insert_w` and `take_front_w`, which count every byte copied inside `Frags`) assume it. Frees are outside the ghost cost: dropping a chunk or a node is not counted; each one `insert` frees (M's drained chunks in `merge`, `Lc`'s old `Vec`) was charged when the same call drained or copied it, an argument by hand, not proved (`cat-C15-semantic-cost`, 9b4). `crypto_ooo_cost_linear` (`tests/crypto_cost.rs`) pins it by counting `realloc` calls and requiring none, on its out-of-order patterns only, and scripts/gate-demos.sh ("crypto_cost: fill's pieces grown from Vec::new()") shows that test failing when `fill` makes its piece without `with_capacity`. `take_front`'s proved cost is O(log n + r) in the bytes r it returns, not in the bytes a frame gave, so in-order delivery is not within the per-call O(d + log n) rule, and it appends to the caller's `out`, whose growth is the caller's and outside this bound (phase 9b2, `cat-C15-9b2`). `Frags::capacity` (per node, the node's size plus its chunk's capacity, in `CryptoStream::capacities`) checks the total in `tests/crypto_stream.rs` (`crypto_ooo_capacity_bounded`). `H3Server`'s pending queue has no proven bound: by construction, not proof, it holds at most one `send_response` response, h3's control writes and at most one h3 answer (a 501 or 421) per open bidi stream (h3_server.rs's module doc), pinned through `H3Server::pending_bytes` by `stopped_answers_do_not_pile_up` in `tests/h3_server.rs`; `tests/h3_alloc.rs` (`zero_room_ping_copies_nothing`) checks with a counting allocator that a flush with no room does not copy the queued tail (under 64 KiB allocated around one PING with about 2 MiB queued).
