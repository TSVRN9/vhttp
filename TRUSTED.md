## Trusted base (from the design spec)

- rustc, Verus (including its `by (compute)` interpreter), Z3, and vstd's specs of std.
- The OS UDP stack, the clock, and getrandom (used for connection IDs, stateless-reset tokens, and each TLS handshake's `TLS_RAND_LEN` bytes: the ServerHello random, the ECDHE scalar and the PSS salt).
- The unverified IO shell and the `main` binary, including loading the certificate chain and parsing the PEM/PKCS#8 P-256 private key and, when configured, the PKCS#8 RSA private key.
- **The hand-written Verus formalization of each RFC** (`spec` modules). Proofs show the code matches these specs; they can't show the specs match the RFC text. Keeping the specs small and readable, with each one citing its RFC section, is how the risk stays manageable.
- vstd models a `Vec`'s length, not its capacity, so every proven memory bound (e.g. `quic::reassembly`'s `phys_len() <= 2 * buf_len()` and `RecvStream`'s `buffered_phys()`) bounds lengths only. `Reassembly::pop` releases both allocations on a full drain by construction (fresh `Vec`s, and `split_off` keeps only the tail allocation); the unverified test-only accessor `Reassembly::capacities` checks it in `tests/reassembly.rs` (`drain_releases_capacity`); likewise `SendStream::on_data_acked` replaces its buffer with a fresh `Vec` once everything written is acknowledged, checked through `SendStream::buf_capacity` in `tests/send_stream.rs` (`ack_releases_capacity`; `ack_copies_linear` uses it to count the bytes its lazy trims copy); and `RecvPns`'s range list holds at most `MAX_ACK_RANGES` + 1 entries at any moment (the lowest is popped in place, never re-allocated), checked through `RecvPns::ranges_capacity` in `tests/ack_track.rs` (`ranges_capacity_bounded`); and `SentPackets::on_ack` and `drain_inflight` leave a fresh `Vec` (the rebuild and the swap), checked through `SentPackets::inflight_capacity` in `tests/sent.rs` (`ack_frees`, `drain_frees`). `SentPackets` itself derives nothing (its ghost `issued` set has no `Debug`). `PeerCids`'s retire lists hold at most `2 * PEER_CID_CAP` entries together (proven in its `wf`), and their capacity is checked through `PeerCids::retire_capacity` in `tests/cid.rs` (`retire_backlog`); its active list holds at most `PEER_CID_CAP` entries (proven), and the Retire Prior To split keeps the tail as a fresh `Vec` (capacity not tested); `PeerCids` derives nothing either (its ghost `received` map and `acked` set have no `Debug`). `CidTable::remove_conn` rebuilds its three lists as fresh `Vec`s (at most `MAX_TABLE` CIDs, `MAX_TOKENS` tokens and `MAX_CONNS` connections, proven in its `wf`), checked through `CidTable::capacities` in `tests/route.rs` (`remove_conn_frees`). `AmpBudget` derives nothing (its ghost trace has no `Debug`), nor do `Path` and `Paths` (they hold one). `Paths` holds at most `MAX_PATHS` paths and each path at most `MAX_RESPONSES` owed responses (proven in its `wf`; the oldest is removed in place), checked through `Paths::capacities` in `tests/path.rs` (`capacity_bounded`). `Connection::discard` gives the discarded space fresh CRYPTO buffers, checked through `Connection::crypto_capacity` (`CryptoStream::capacities` plus the send buffer's `buf_capacity`) in `tests/conn_rx.rs` (`handshake_validates_and_discards` for Initial, `discard_frees_crypto` for Handshake). `H3Server`'s pending queue has no proven bound: by construction, not proof, it holds at most one `send_response` response, h3's control writes and at most one h3 answer (a 501 or 421) per open bidi stream (h3_server.rs's module doc), pinned through `H3Server::pending_bytes` by `stopped_answers_do_not_pile_up` in `tests/h3_server.rs`; `tests/h3_alloc.rs` (`zero_room_ping_copies_nothing`) checks with a counting allocator that a flush with no room does not copy the queued tail (under 64 KiB allocated around one PING with about 2 MiB queued).
